About the school

Mathematics behind meaningful images

Image segmentation separates an image into regions that can be analysed and interpreted. M2IP 2027 introduces early-career researchers to the mathematical ideas behind these methods and the practical decisions that influence their results.

Guided derivations, numerical exercises and mentored investigations connect model assumptions with working algorithms. Participants study a focused set of methods and examine when, why and how their results change.

01 · FORMULATE

Connect mathematics with implementation

Translate segmentation objectives and update equations into numerical experiments.

02 · INVESTIGATE

Understand the behaviour of methods

Examine the effects of assumptions, noise, initialisation and parameter choices.

03 · EVALUATE

Conduct focused comparisons

Use fair baselines, appropriate metrics and reproducible experimental settings.

04 · CONTINUE

Develop a research direction

Refine findings and project reports through a 30-day mentored follow-up.

Syllabus

A focused progression from mathematical foundations to reproducible segmentation experiments.

Module 01 · 3 hours

Mathematical Foundations

Discrete images, sampling, quantisation, pixel neighbourhoods, convolution, smoothing and gradients. Segmentation as a partition of the image domain.

Module 02 · 3 hours

Classical Segmentation

Global thresholding, Otsu’s criterion, region growing, reference masks and baseline evaluation.

Module 03 · 6 hours

Clustering and Optimisation

K-means objectives and alternating updates. Initialisation and convergence. Fuzzy C-means, constrained optimisation, membership updates and the fuzzifier.

Module 04 · 6 hours

Spatial Models and Regularisation

Intensity fidelity, neighbourhood penalties, two-label energies and single-pixel updates. Effects on boundaries and small regions.

Module 05 · 6 hours

Experimental Design and Validation

Fair baselines, noise and parameter sensitivity, Dice, IoU, boundary error, tuning and testing, repeated trials and variability.

Module 06 · 3 hours

Reproducible Research

Dataset provenance, code organisation, settings and random seeds. Mathematical exposition, interpretation, limitations and continuation.

Applications & participation

Begin your application

Who can apply?

  • Postgraduate students, research scholars, postdoctoral researchers and early-career faculty in mathematics or related disciplines.
  • Basic knowledge of linear algebra, calculus and probability.
  • Ability to write simple Python or MATLAB programs.
  • Prior research experience in image processing is not required.
  • Commitment to the full school and the 30-day project follow-up.

Selection criteria

  • Academic merit and a statement of interest of 150–200 words.
  • The statement should cover academic background, research interests, reasons for attending and expected benefits.
  • Preference for postgraduate students, research scholars and early-career researchers below 35 years of age.
  • Among equally suitable applicants, limited access to similar training and institutional and geographical diversity will be considered.
  • 40 participants will be selected. A reserve list will be maintained.

Research Outcomes & Awards

  • A precise problem statement and mathematical formulation.
  • Documented implementation and reproducible settings.

  • A realistic research continuation plan.

Useful information

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need prior research experience in image processing?

No. The school welcomes beginners with the stated mathematical and programming background.

Should I pay when submitting my application?

No. Only selected candidates pay the ₹2,000 registration fee after receiving instructions.

Are the fee refund and travel support guaranteed?

No. Both depend on funding availability and the communicated conditions. Fee-refund eligibility also requires at least 90% attendance and completion of assessments.

Will accommodation be available?

Availability and charges will be included in the selection notice. Free accommodation depends on funding availability.

Is project work required after the school?

Yes. Participants should commit to the 30-day follow-up and final report submission.

Is publication guaranteed?

No. Selected work may be considered after revision and editorial review, subject to publisher acceptance.
